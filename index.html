Two ready-made weekdays

Suggestions to start from, not rules. Homeschooling in England has no set hours; what matters is that it is full-time and suits each child. Both keep English and maths in the morning, when attention is best, and leave afternoons for projects and being outside.

Taking a child out of school in England

Parents can choose to homeschool their children (the law calls it “elective home education”). You don't have to follow the national curriculum, but the education must be efficient, full-time and suitable for the child's age, ability and any special needs.

If your child is registered at a school, write to the head teacher to say you're taking them out to homeschool. The school tells the council, and the council may ask how you're educating them. A special school needs the council's agreement first.

Before deciding, read the official guidance and talk to your council: gov.uk/home-education.

Homeschooling in the United States

There's no national curriculum in the US, and homeschooling law is set by each state: some only ask you to tell the school district, others want test results or a portfolio. Check your own state's rules before you start – HSLDA keeps a plain summary for every state: hslda.org/legal.

Kindergarten to Grade 5 here are the Common Core State Standards for English Language Arts and Mathematics, word for word. Most states use them, or a version very close; a few (for example Texas, Virginia and Florida) set their own. US grades run about a year older than England's years: a Grade 3 child is usually the age of a Year 4 child in England.

What's on this site, and where it comes from

Year 1 to Year 6 English and maths are the statutory requirements from The national curriculum in England: primary curriculum (Department for Education), word for word. English is set for each year in Years 1 and 2, then for two years at a time: Years 3 and 4 share the Years 3–4 programme, and Years 5 and 6 share the Years 5–6 programme. Maths is set for each year.

Reception shows the early learning goals from the Early years foundation stage statutory framework for group and school-based providers, effective from September 2026. They describe the expected level at the end of Reception; the framework says they shouldn't be used as a curriculum, so treat them as a guide to where children are heading.

The spelling lists are the statutory word lists for Years 3–4 and Years 5–6.
